Verdicts

The answer for each token

YES means measured demand for the token keeps up with new supply. NO means it does not keep up with new supply or with network use. MIXED means some real demand, but not enough for a clear call. NOT ENOUGH DATA means the data needed is missing or not public. Rules are fixed in advance and applied the same way every day.

Chainlink (LINK)

New LINK released vs LINK bought into the Reserve

Chainlink converts part of its revenue into LINK and parks it in the Reserve. That is the demand side. Supply comes from LINK released out of Chainlink's locked wallets, mostly in big quarterly unlocks.

LINK bought into the Reserve, each time

Chainlink tops up the Reserve about once a week. Bigger and more frequent bars would mean more revenue turning into LINK demand.

Quant (QNT)

What we can and can’t see

QNT is meant to be used two ways: companies pay Overledger licence fees in QNT, and apps pay for Fusion (Quant’s own network) with QNT. Neither shows up on a public ledger yet, so this section shows what is visible and says plainly what is not.

Supply (fixed)

QNT has a fixed supply and no published unlock schedule like LINK's quarterly releases, so there is no new-supply number to compare against. The question is only whether anyone spends or locks QNT to use the network.

Can we see it?

  • YES · QNT supply, holders and transfers on Ethereum (Blockscout).
  • NO · Licence fees paid in QNT. Quant has not published the treasury contract they go into.
  • NO · Fusion gas paid in QNT. No public Fusion mainnet explorer or deposit contract. Our Overledger account can only read Fusion testnet.
  • NO · Staking. Not open to the public.

The day Quant publishes the treasury or Fusion deposit address, the sheet will start counting it, and the Quant gauge will get a needle.

Methodology

How the numbers are made

  1. Burned CC is the sum of every account's burns per UTC day (Lighthouse, a copy of Canton Scan). The source lists the top 50 accounts per window, so busy days are split into 6-hour, then 1-hour windows until every account is counted. If a 1-hour window is still full, that day is marked as a lower bound.
  2. Created CC is app + validator + super validator rewards for every round (about 144 a day). The development fund (about 5%) is not reported per day, so it is left out and listed as a gap.
  3. Who burns compares each top account's 30-day burns with the app rewards it earned. Names are matched against the institutions in the tokenization claims tracker.
  4. LINK Reserve inflows come from the Reserve contract on Ethereum, released supply from Chainlink's non-circulating wallets, and usage from the CCIP API.
  5. CCIP fee token is read from every send event on Chainlink's Ethereum OnRamp contracts (Blockscout). Each event names the token that paid the fee. ETH paid as a fee is wrapped to WETH, so WETH counts as ETH. Daily totals match the CCIP API's count of messages sent from Ethereum.
  6. QNT transfers are the day-to-day change in Blockscout’s running count of QNT transfers, read once a day and scaled to 24 hours. Supply comes straight from the QNT contract. Licence cost = £100 × the ECB pound-to-dollar rate ÷ the QNT price.
  7. Verdicts use fixed thresholds written down before any data was collected. Missing data is never treated as zero; it makes the answer MIXED or NOT ENOUGH DATA instead.

    Glossary

    How to read this

    Burn
    Canton Coin destroyed to pay for using the network. Real use means burning.
    Created (minted)
    New Canton Coin paid out every ~10 minutes as rewards to apps, validators and super validators.
    Reward-funded
    An account that earns at least as much in app rewards as it burns. Its burning can pay for itself.
    Net payer
    An account whose rewards cover less than half of what it burns. The closest thing to real outside demand.
    Chainlink Reserve
    An on-chain wallet where Chainlink converts revenue (including fees paid in other tokens) into LINK.
    Released LINK
    LINK moved out of Chainlink's locked wallets into circulation.
    CCIP
    Chainlink's cross-chain messaging network. Message counts show how much it is used.
    Overledger / Fusion
    Quant’s software (Overledger) and its own network (Fusion). Licences are priced in pounds and paid in QNT; Fusion uses QNT for fees.
    CIP-0129
    Approved Oct 21, 2026: makes private Canton networks pay in burned CC. Expected around Q1 2027, which is when bank use could start to show here.
